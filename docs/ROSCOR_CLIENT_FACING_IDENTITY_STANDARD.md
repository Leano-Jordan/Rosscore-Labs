# Rosscore Labs — Client-Facing Identity & Commercial Presentation Standard

**Status:** Mandatory company-wide policy  
**Owner:** Founder  
**Applies to:** Ross, Jarvis, Swifty, Gnu, ITC/Jodie, Mags, Cater, all future Directors and specialists, and all customer-facing material across Rosscore Labs projects.

## 1. Company and founder are separate identities

Rosscore Labs is the business. The Founder owns and operates the business; the Founder is not the business's customer-facing identity.

Products must be presented as professional products offered by Rosscore Labs, not as personal projects that customers are being asked to support. A prospect should be able to assess a product on its own merits without feeling that a purchase, signup, compliment, form submission, or referral is a personal favour to the Founder.

## 2. Mandatory customer-facing posture

For product pages, pre-launch pages, websites, proposals, demos, outreach, social posts, onboarding, sales copy and customer communications:

- Lead with the product, its intended customer, its business value and its availability.
- Present Rosscore Labs as the company behind the product where company attribution is relevant.
- Use a confident, neutral, commercially credible SaaS/business tone.
- Give prospective customers a low-pressure way to independently review the offer and decide whether to enquire, request a demo, register interest or do nothing.
- Treat calls to action as commercial choices, not requests for emotional support, validation, encouragement or participation in the Founder's personal journey.
- Do not make customers responsible for shaping, rescuing, validating or helping a solo developer unless the Founder explicitly approves a genuine customer co-design programme as a commercial offer.
- Avoid unnecessary disclosure of the Founder's personal circumstances, employment status, development setup or staffing arrangements in customer-facing material.
- Keep the personal relationship separate from the sales relationship; friends and family are not to be treated as validated leads merely because they submit a form or offer encouragement.

## 3. Truthfulness — professional presentation is not fabrication

Commercial polish must never be achieved through false claims. Do not invent or imply employee counts, departments, offices, customers, testimonials, partnerships, certifications, funding, market adoption, product availability, guaranteed access, scarcity or delivery capacity.

Do not claim Rosscore Labs has 100 employees. The Founder’s preferred customer-facing posture is a company-led professional presentation that does not volunteer internal staffing details, not a fabricated headcount. If a customer, contract, law or due-diligence process requires a material disclosure, answer accurately.

Product status, functionality, pricing, support, security, privacy, offline behaviour and release timing must remain accurate and evidence-based.

## 4. Early access and pre-launch pages

Early access should be positioned as an opportunity for the prospect to gain an early look at a developing product and, where genuinely available, request a demonstration or register interest in future availability.

Preferred direction:
- “Request early access”
- “Explore a product demo”
- “Register your interest”
- “Be among the first to explore upcoming releases” (only when this accurately describes the programme)

Avoid defaulting to:
- “Support my project”
- “Help me build this”
- “Help shape my product”
- “Join the first conversations”
- wording that makes the prospect feel obliged to provide feedback or do the Founder a favour.

Do not promise priority, exclusivity, special features, limited places, influence over the roadmap or guaranteed access unless the programme actually provides it and the Founder has approved that commitment.

Forms should request only information needed to handle the prospect's chosen enquiry. Optional feedback must remain optional and must not feel like the price of entry.

## 5. Outreach and distribution

The objective of marketing is to put the product in front of relevant prospective customers so they can make an independent decision. The page or sales material should do the explaining; the prospect should not be pressured to praise, submit a form, or continue a conversation.

Respect applicable privacy, direct-marketing, platform and anti-spam requirements. A public business listing is not blanket permission for mass unsolicited marketing.

## 6. Internal documentation versus external presentation

Internal company documents may accurately state that Rosscore Labs is currently Founder-led or operated by a solo developer when this is material to capacity, risk, hiring, finance or strategy. Such operational facts must not automatically leak into customer-facing copy.

Every Director must preserve this distinction and must not remove truthful internal operational context merely to make internal documentation sound larger.

## 7. Director enforcement

Before accepting customer-facing copy, each Director must check:

1. Does it lead with the product/company rather than the Founder personally?
2. Could a reasonable prospect feel they are being asked to support a person instead of evaluating a product?
3. Does it allow the prospect to decline silently and comfortably?
4. Are all company-size, customer, capability, access and availability claims true and evidenced?
5. Are feedback and discovery requests optional rather than emotionally or procedurally coercive?
6. Does the material maintain the relevant product's actual scope and target audience?

If the material fails, revise it before publication. Do not require the Founder to repeat this instruction in each project conversation.

## 8. Authority

This is a canonical company-wide rule. All Rosscore Labs Directors and specialists must follow it alongside their active repository contract. Project-specific instructions may strengthen it, but must not contradict it. The Founder may amend this policy explicitly.
